Comparing Social Media Schedulers? Here's What Actually Differs.

Most schedulers do the same basic thing: write a post, pick a time, it goes out automatically. Here's where they actually differ, in pricing and in what happens after you hit schedule.

The gap that actually matters: "sent" isn't "published"

Every scheduler we've looked at, including the most popular per-channel ones, stops at the same point: it calls the platform's API, gets back a success response, and marks your post "sent." That response is a receipt that your request was accepted. It is not proof the post is actually visible to anyone.

In between, real things can and do go wrong: rate limits, moderation holds, an expired access token, a platform having a partial outage while still accepting requests. None of these show up as an obvious error. Your dashboard still says "posted." The post itself might not exist.

LazyRelay doesn't stop at "accepted." After every post goes out, it separately re-checks the platform to confirm the post is genuinely live. That's Proof-of-Publish. If that check fails, the post is flagged as unverified in your dashboard instead of being silently marked successful. As far as we've found, this isn't something the popular per-channel tools do at all.

The other real difference: how the bill grows

A per-channel pricing model is cheap when you're running two or three accounts. It stops being cheap the moment you're managing a real number of them: the price scales with every account you connect, not with what you're actually using the tool for. LazyRelay's plans are priced in credits, at 1 credit per channel destination for each post, so the bill follows how much you publish, not how many pages you connect.

Frequently asked questions

Does a typical social media scheduler tell me if my post silently failed to publish?

No. Most schedulers only confirm that the platform's API accepted the request, then stop checking. If the platform later rejects, holds, or drops the post, the scheduler has no way of knowing, and neither do you, unless you check the account by hand.

Is LazyRelay actually cheaper than a per-channel scheduler?

It depends on how many accounts you run. For two or three channels, a per-channel tool can be cheaper. LazyRelay is priced in credits instead, at 1 credit per channel destination for each post, so what you pay follows how much you publish, not how many accounts you connect.

What is Proof-of-Publish?

After LazyRelay sends a scheduled post, it separately re-checks the platform to confirm the post is genuinely live, rather than trusting the initial "accepted" response. If verification fails, the post is flagged in your dashboard instead of being silently marked successful.
